Saved literature summary

The manuscript’s central proposal is a mechanical interpretation of an established gravitational boundary response: use the clock-normalized acceleration length L=c2/κL=c^2/\kappaL=c2/κ as a work coordinate, identify its conjugate force FL=MUκF_L=M_U\kappaFL​=MU​κ, and interpret FL/AF_L/AFL​/A as a normal-scale pressure. This is more specific than a restatement of U=TSU=TSU=TS, and it could offer a useful conceptual advance within Einstein gravity. However, earlier supplied passages already derive the same area-weighted surface-gravity variation and its canonical/thermal meaning. The potentially distinctive element is therefore the geometrically motivated length-coordinate interpretation and its pressure/work translations, not the underlying action coefficient. The supplied mathematical report supports the declared scalar identities, but not their operational interpretation, uniqueness, or applicability across physical regimes. The Schwarzschild results are conditional consequences; the FRW construction supplies a different screen-pressure interpretation, not new cosmological dynamics. Overall, the evidence supports a partly distinctive interpretation with substantial prior foundations, while leaving exact priority and the physical status of the proposed force unresolved.

Scientific review — What the proposal means

The clearest reading is that the paper gives two mechanical descriptions of changes in a horizon boundary potential. Increasing area at fixed acceleration length increases UUU, with coefficient Π\PiΠ. Increasing acceleration length at fixed area decreases UUU, with coefficient −FL-F_L−FL​. In Rindler geometry, acceleration length has the stated interpretation as the observer’s normal distance to the horizon on an instantaneous rest slice. This motivates treating its variation as a mechanical displacement rather than merely as a change of inverse temperature.

The pressure distinction is essential. Π\PiΠ has units of energy per area, or force per length: it is a two-dimensional surface stress. PL=Π/LP_L=\Pi/LPL​=Π/L has ordinary three-dimensional pressure units. The proposed normal-scale pressure is not simply another name for membrane surface pressure. Moreover, ΠdA\Pi dAΠdA and AdΠ=−FLdLA d\Pi=-F_LdLAdΠ=−FL​dL are different responses on the independent-coordinate space; one cannot replace area work by length work without specifying which variation is being considered.

Thermal language supplies a useful translation, but is not needed for the derivative-defined force:
ΠdA=TdS,AdΠ=SdT=−FLdL.\Pi dA=T dS,\qquad A d\Pi=S dT=-F_LdL.ΠdA=TdS,AdΠ=SdT=−FL​dL.
The second relation exposes the closest connection to earlier action calculations: the manuscript proposes a mechanical length-displacement interpretation of the already established area-weighted surface-gravity variation.

The spherical examples require a further distinction between a partial derivative and a derivative along a solution family. For Schwarzschild, area and length co-vary. The complete differential remains the sum of both contributions; FLdLF_LdLFL​dL alone is not dUdUdU. Since dL=2dRdL=2dRdL=2dR and dV=A dRdV=A\,dRdV=AdR, preserving this work gives PR=2PLP_R=2P_LPR​=2PL​. The resulting Mc2=TS+FLLMc^2=TS+F_LLMc2=TS+FL​L consists of two equal products, each UUU; it does not by itself establish two independently exchangeable energy reservoirs.

For FRW, the length-radius conversion is instead unity, so P=PLP=P_LP=PL​. The identity P=ρ/3P=\rho/3P=ρ/3 concerns a screen response defined from the assigned geometry and energy. It is not a radiation equation of state for the bulk: the manuscript explicitly keeps bulk pressure pmp_mpm​ separate. Its acceleration and conservation equations retain that distinction. Evolving FRW is expressly not derived by placing the stationary null-sector response on a stationary worldtube.

Scientific review — Established foundations and direct overlaps

The closest precedent is not merely the formula TSTSTS. Parattu et al. (2013 preprint, selected passages) derive a horizon surface Hamiltonian with that value and split its variation into a metric contribution and a conjugate-momentum contribution. Their displayed horizon integrals give both κδA/(8π)\kappa\delta A/(8\pi)κδA/(8π) and Aδκ/(8π)A\delta\kappa/(8\pi)Aδκ/(8π) in their units. With conventions matched, the latter is precisely the coefficient from which the present manuscript constructs −FLδL-F_L\delta L−FL​δL. Their variations preserve the near-horizon metric structure and compare horizon configurations; they are not an unrestricted displacement theorem. Thus the action origin, coefficient, and separation of the two response channels have substantial earlier precedent.

Hopfmüller–Freidel (2016 preprint, selected passages) derive the null-boundary action, corner contributions, and spin-zero conjugacy involving area and surface gravity/expansion. They explicitly distinguish a potential containing δκ\delta\kappaδκ from a polarization in which it is absorbed into an exact variation and the area response. They also hold the boundary location fixed while varying its geometry. These are direct foundations for the manuscript’s canonical interpretation, and demonstrate why action and polarization must be specified.

The 2023 null-charge paper displays several polarizations. In the expansion-free scalar restriction, its CFPk and conformal forms contain 2δk2\delta k2δk times the null measure, whereas its CS form contains δk\delta kδk together with an area-measure variation. Consequently neither nonexpansion alone nor the word “mixed” uniquely determines the manuscript’s coefficient. The supplied passages confirm the relevant response structures, but not the manuscript’s complete prescription.

Earlier membrane calculations obtain surface stresses from a stretched-horizon action variation and a redshift-regulated stress tensor. Kolekar–Kothawala’s 2011 preprint, published in JHEP in 2012, supplies the Einstein-limit relation between membrane surface pressure, area, and horizon heat content. This overlaps Π\PiΠ, not automatically the three-dimensional PLP_LPL​. Its operational source is a membrane stress tensor rather than the inverse-surface-gravity displacement proposed here.

Cosmological horizon thermodynamics also predates this manuscript. Scientific review — Precisely what might advance the subject

The strongest candidate advance is the identification of L=c2/κL=c^2/\kappaL=c2/κ as a geometrically motivated mechanical coordinate, with an action-supported conjugate force and a normal-scale force density. This should not be dismissed simply because it introduces neither a new interaction nor new Einstein equations. If an admissible geometric variation and an operational response can be specified, recognizing an existing canonical response as length work could change how horizon mechanics is interpreted.

The distinction must nevertheless be narrow. Parattu et al. already derive the same area-weighted δκ\delta\kappaδκ coefficient from a gravitational momentum variation, not just from multiplying temperature and entropy. Hopfmüller–Freidel and the 2023 null-charge paper already explain the underlying conjugacy and polarization dependence. The manuscript adds the inverse-surface-gravity coordinate map, its Rindler distance motivation, and the force/pressure identification. On the supplied passages this is a partly distinctive interpretation of an earlier response, not a newly discovered action term or an additional canonical degree of freedom. Those excerpts do not establish whether the same length interpretation occurs elsewhere in the complete papers or wider literature.

The critical physical question is what δL\delta LδL represents. The prior null-boundary calculations vary geometry on a fixed boundary. The manuscript describes horizon-scale change, but has not supplied a general construction showing when this parameter variation is a measurable normal displacement while preserving its clock, cuts, and allowed variations. Rindler gives its best local geometric motivation. Schwarzschild’s L=2RL=2RL=2R, with time normalized at infinity, is a scale relation rather than a claim that LLL is a proper radial distance. Comparing these meanings is necessary before asserting a common mechanical operation.

The Schwarzschild factor PR/PL=2P_R/P_L=2PR​/PL​=2 is a sound conditional consequence of the chosen coordinate conversion. Its explanatory interest is that work is preserved while force and pressure coefficients change. The mass-energy sum adds a mechanical reading to two equal products, but no independent partition or new first law is established.

The FRW pressure is a second potentially useful interpretation: it connects the assigned normal-scale force to P=ρ/3P=\rho/3P=ρ/3, distinct from both bulk pressure and Akbar–Cai work density. This differs substantively in coefficient, temperature prescription, and response interpretation. It must be presented as a separate equilibrium-screen construction, not as confirmation of the full dynamical unified first law or of Wang’s membrane stress.

No new testable empirical prediction is supplied. The force and pressure coefficients are theoretical consistency targets conditional on the adopted prescriptions. The printed cosmological equations are explicitly Einstein–FRW equations in new variables, so their agreement with observations would not discriminate this interpretation from Einstein gravity.

Scientific review — Explanatory value and wider contribution

Established layer. The supplied literature already connects gravitational boundary actions, area/surface-gravity conjugacy, membrane stresses, horizon heat content, and apparent-horizon cosmology. The manuscript does not replace those explanations or derive their foundations anew.

Proposed layer. Its most useful contribution could be a precise bridge between the canonical surface-gravity response and a length-work description. In that bridge, Π\PiΠ describes an area response, FLF_LFL​ a normal-scale response, and PLP_LPL​ its force per area. Keeping these quantities distinct could clarify why different horizon “pressures” have different dimensions and coefficients. The Schwarzschild and FRW examples make a concrete point: choosing L=2RL=2RL=2R versus L=RAL=R_AL=RA​ changes radial work coefficients without changing the work itself. That is a potentially valuable conceptual clarification, rather than evidence for an extra interaction.

The advance remains conditional on showing that the geometrically normalized coordinate is physically privileged for the stated response, not just convenient. Existing action/polarization analyses make this a substantive requirement. If established, the proposal would enrich the mechanical interpretation of a known sector of Einstein gravity. It would not increase the number of boundary degrees of freedom or alter the bulk equations.

The cosmological payoff is currently interpretive. P=ρ/3P=\rho/3P=ρ/3 offers a screen-response variable while pmp_mpm​ continues to control the matter contribution to acceleration and conservation. This could help organize comparisons between equilibrium-screen mechanics and dynamical horizon thermodynamics, but does not explain a new source of cosmic acceleration, determine vacuum energy, or supply a new matter equation of state.

Speculative extensions—such as an independently measurable horizon force, a unique physical energy partition, or applications to general evolving boundaries—are not established here. No new observational prediction is provided. The field contribution should therefore be framed as a bounded, potentially insightful mechanical identification with checked scalar consequences, not as demonstrated new gravitational dynamics or certified originality.

Scientific review — Mathematical, physical, and evidence limits

1. Boundary prescription and coefficient. The page does not provide the complete bulk/boundary/corner action, orientation conventions, normalization of the null measure, or exact polarization defining its “mixed” prescription. The displayed earlier polarizations differ in their δk\delta kδk and area terms. A derivation must show which prescription yields the printed coefficient and what is retained or removed by exact variations and corners. The scalar restriction must also explain how shear and generator-variation sectors are excluded. Harlow–Wu, the relative-bicomplex treatment, and the null-boundary analyses show why these details matter; a scalar coefficient match is not a complete covariant-phase-space derivation.

2. Admissible displacement and clock dependence. Independent A,LA,LA,L variations are useful for the potential calculation, but their realization by allowed metric variations requires demonstration. A fixed boundary with varying geometry is not automatically a displaced boundary. Rindler’s distance interpretation does not remove observer/time-normalization dependence for arbitrary horizons. The force and pressure should remain explicitly attached to their clock and boundary prescription.

3. Physical interpretation. Differentiating the adopted potential establishes a generalized response by definition, not a measured stress or a new force law. Likewise, U=TSU=TSU=TS does not automatically make UUU an internal energy for every thermodynamic process. The Schwarzschild sum requires an independent physical accounting argument if it is intended as more than two equal products adding to the stipulated mass energy.

4. Evolving FRW. The page appropriately separates its instantaneous screen assignment from the stationary null sector. Nevertheless, the range of validity of its positive inverse-radius temperature and L=RAL=R_AL=RA​ assignment is not quantified. Cai–Kim discuss the approximation involved in a dynamical apparent-horizon temperature; Akbar–Cai use a different work density and surface-gravity prescription; Wang finds a dynamical membrane-pressure correction. These cannot be identified with the manuscript’s PPP without an explicit translation. The scalar first-law identity does not derive the acceleration or conservation laws from the boundary potential.
